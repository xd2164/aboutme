About

Building the future of AI personalization across product, interaction, and infrastructure.

I believe the next major shift in AI will come from systems that become more useful through interaction over time—understanding context, adapting to changing goals, and personalizing without becoming opaque, intrusive, or overly controlling.

I am a Senior Program Officer at the Gates Foundation's US Programs, where I support AI strategy, infrastructure, and deployment for K–12 and postsecondary education. I work alongside foundation colleagues, university researchers, and AI companies including Anthropic to build the field conditions for responsible, equitable AI adoption in education.

My research centers on AI literacy—what it means to understand AI well enough to question it—and how to design systems that build that capacity rather than bypass it. I have published two books (Corwin 2025, Springer 2026) and peer-reviewed papers with co-authors from MIT Media Lab, Columbia University, and Teachers College. My work spans curriculum frameworks, game-based learning, professional development, and agentic AI safety.

At the product level, I build and evaluate AI experiences that translate personalization into real user value. Through Humanly, I explore how AI-assisted experiences can remain configurable, traceable, and user-controlled; through my work in AI literacy and learning products, I focus on systems that support reflection, agency, and deeper learning rather than simply faster answers.

At the human-AI interaction level, my research at the MIT Media Lab examined how people understand, collaborate with, and build trust with AI systems. That work continues to shape my product philosophy: personalization should reflect how people actually think, learn, change their minds, and develop relationships with intelligent systems over time.

At the AI infrastructure level, I contribute to the Human Context Protocol (MIT, Stanford, Oxford — Shah et al.), an open architecture for user-governed AI memory and personalization. I also model frontier agentic AI safety risks applied to education infrastructure, and serve as external evaluator for the NSF-funded Science Pathways Scholars Program (SP)² at Barnard College (Award 2030903).

I hold an EdD from Teachers College, Columbia University.
